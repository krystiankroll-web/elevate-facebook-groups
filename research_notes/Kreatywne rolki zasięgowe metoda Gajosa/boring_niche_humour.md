## Q1. Which Polish companies in unglamorous industries have viral humorous reels/TikToks, and in what formats?

### Takeaway
Documented Polish brand cases in "boring" categories are few. **WIŚNIOWSKI** (gates/doors manufacturer) is the closest to a building-materials producer: ~2.9M views on top product clips (reported by its agency). **Wuber** (DIY tools) used humour + POV and hit ROAS targets, also agency-reported. A small funeral home, **Zakład Pogrzebowy A.S. Bytom**, runs dark self-irony. The biggest Polish "industrial humour" reach on YouTube Shorts comes from **creator-run parody characters**, not companies: Raptus (transport/construction-boss parody, Shorts up to 1.5M) and HRejterzy (office/HR skits, Shorts 1–2M). Big retail/logistics brands (Castorama, Leroy Merlin, InPost, Żabka) get their YouTube reach from ads, tutorials and series, not humour Shorts.

### Cited Findings
**WIŚNIOWSKI (Polish manufacturer of garage doors, gates, fences): building industry on TikTok**
- Agency case study "Czy TikTok to narzędzie dla branży budowlanej?" Official account ~43.7k followers and ~795.4k likes; the most popular videos featuring the **MultiBox** product (a parcel box built into the fence) got ~2.9M views. Agency-published, no breakdown of humour vs. non-humour, roughly 2024 (search tool said "~659 days old"). — [PRoto.pl case study](https://www.proto.pl/case-studies/czy-tiktok-to-narzedzie-dla-branzy-budowlanej-case-study-marki-wisniowski/) (via search summary; page not fetchable)
- The profile uses industry hashtags with tongue-in-cheek captions, e.g. "Coś co totalnie kładzie na łopatki… 🤫😱 #bramy #bramagarażowa". Also topical days such as "Dzień Bramy Garażowej". — [TikTok @wisniowski_official video](https://www.tiktok.com/@wisniowski_official/video/7418638867458297110), [profile](https://www.tiktok.com/@wisniowski_official)
- Mechanism (inference from the product): MultiBox is a "product solves an everyday annoyance" item (couriers, parcels). That is relatable pain, not pure comedy.

**Wuber / Wuber Tools (Polish DIY tools e-commerce): "wiedza, humor i produkt"**

## Q2. International examples in boring/industrial niches: numbers and what made them funny

### Takeaway
The strongest international patterns are:
- a **personified brand or mascot with a mean or self-deprecating voice** (Ryanair, Duolingo, Scrub Daddy);
- **office-culture personalities at a B2B manufacturer** (VILPE);
- **accidental earnestness turned meme** (Donghua Jinlong glycine).

Product-test content in the client's own category (gym flooring drop tests) gets only hundreds to low thousands of views on YouTube. Straight "proof" content alone does not travel.

### Cited Findings
**VILPE (Finnish roof-ventilation manufacturer, B2B): the canonical "boring B2B on TikTok" case**
- Grew to 80k followers in under 12 months. A podcast episode covers how it went from 20k to 80k in eight weeks, and the host says it produced "more job applications than ever before" (claimed, not quantified). — [B2B Better / B2B Bite](https://b2bbite.substack.com/p/roofing-specialist-tiktok)
- Agency blog: more than 100k followers. "The vast majority follow them for their office culture"; the account works by "shining a light on the personalities behind the brand". A TikTok discover page lists ~104.8k followers. — [Sculpt: B2B TikTok examples](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/), [TikTok discover: vilpe](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/vilpe?lang=en)
- Headline from roughly 2022: "How a Roofing Specialist Grew Its TikTok Following by 300% in Eight Weeks". — [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tuula-liikanen_how-a-roofing-specialist-grew-its-tiktok-activity-6973704629599514624-YP2R)
- VILPE's YouTube is product-led (e.g. "SK Tuote – Solar power plant", ~1.2k views), which shows the reach came from personality content on TikTok, not product films. — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wbe4J8Dn3cI)
- Mark: the core VILPE growth is from **2022 (older classic)**.

**Ryanair (budget airline, "dull/hated" category): personified plane, roasting customers**
- YouTube Shorts measured 2026-10-10: "yes we do 💸 #stitch" ~2.0M ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/XPQdRxm5wU0)); "buy your own plane 🥱 #stitch" ~590k ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/zsNb49CFVO0)); "NO JEANS! #stitch" ~433k ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/uXMICK9BJZg)); "expectations too high" ~319k ([link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/m0xe4nrjiXM)).
- Mechanism: **stitching customer complaints** and answering with deadpan self-awareness ("expectations too high", "buy your own plane"). The brand owns its weakness (cheap = no frills) instead of hiding it.
- SocialChain credits the mascot/plane-face TikTok with 11M views on one piece of content and describes the tone as "personifying the Ryanair plane". — [SocialChain: brands need to lose their corporate tone of voice](https://www.socialchain.com/social-minds/insights/brands-need-to-lose-their-corporate-tone-of-voice-to-succeed-on-social/)
- Skift (Oct 2025) profiles CMO Dara Brady's irreverent, sometimes controversial voice and turning memes into core messaging without heavy ad spend. — [Skift](https://skift.com/2025/10/18/from-tanks-to-tiktok-inside-ryanairs-cheeky-marketing-strategy/)
- The Drum (Nov 2023): "the art and rules of going gonzo on TikTok". — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2023/11/10/lessons-ryanair-the-art-and-rules-going-gonzo-tiktok)

**Duolingo (app, but the model for a mascot-led unhinged voice)**
- "Dead Duo" stunt, Feb 2025: Fast Company reports a record 1.7B impressions across socials in two weeks. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91313082/duolingo-dead-duo-owl-social-media-campaign)

**Donghua Jinlong (Chinese industrial glycine plant): accidental earnest-B2B meme**
- Posted machine-translated, AI-voiced, CapCut-style promo videos from 3 Dec 2023, with captions like "feel free to inquire". Western TikTok turned it into a meme in early 2024. One parody reached 124.8k plays in two days.

It says one marketing firm serving factories saw client inquiries triple, but the scope of that claim is unclear. — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/04/28/1115876/why-chinese-manufacturers-are-going-viral-on-tiktok/) (via search summary)

**Baseline in the client's own category (gym/rubber flooring drop tests on YouTube)**
- Typical reach is tiny: Fit Pit "(Drop Test) For those worried about gym flooring" ~1.4k; Greatmats ~500; Adanac "Smash Test" ~320; Sprung "20KG DROP TEST" ~23; sofSURFACES ~1.2k. — yt-dlp search "rubber gym flooring drop test", e.g. [Fit Pit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COLPx2kq5vs), [Adanac](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fuwAKQiXg64)

### Inferences
- Straight product demos in flooring/building materials sit at 10²–10⁴ views. The documented 10⁵–10⁶ results all add a **character or voice** (plane face, owl, sponge, office personalities, a parody boss) on top of the product. This matches the client's own 10–100x gap between gags and talking heads.
- Hart Tools was named in the brief, but search returned only third-party reviews (e.g. "I Bought Every HART Tool at Walmart", ~2.5M, by a creator). No brand-made humour data found.

### Gaps
- No verified per-video TikTok numbers for VILPE, Ryanair or Scrub Daddy on TikTok (TikTok not readable). YouTube Shorts figures are used as a proxy.
- Hart Tools brand TikTok: nothing found.
- No flooring or epoxy company with documented comedy virality found.

## Q3. Which humour devices recur?

### Takeaway
Recurring devices, mapped to the cases above:
1. **Self-deprecation / owning the weakness**: Ryanair "expectations too high", the Bytom funeral home, self-irony scoring top engagement in the UW study.
2. **Personified product or mascot**: Ryanair plane, Duo, Scrub Daddy.
3. **Stitch/reply to a real customer comment or complaint**: Ryanair stitches.
4. **Boss/employee workplace archetypes**: Raptus, HRejterzy, VILPE office culture.
5. **Expectation vs reality**: Raptus, Bytom.
6. **Behind-the-scenes "a day at work" of a non-human or unglamorous role**: Duo's Work Day, Scrub Daddy's Morning Routine.
7. **Deadpan earnestness**: glycine "feel free to inquire".
8. **Absurd escalation/stunt**: Dead Duo.

### Cited Findings
- Self-ironic humour produced the highest engagement among four humour types in a brand-TikTok study. — [UW repository](https://repozytorium.uw.edu.pl/items/7e5dc5a2-bbc6-47b3-95a6-90ae3a9a78ec/full)
- Ryanair's top Shorts are stitches answering customers ("yes we do", "NO JEANS!", "buy your own plane"). — [YouTube Ryanair Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/XPQdRxm5wU0)
- Losing the corporate tone of voice and personifying the product is credited for Ryanair's results. — [SocialChain](https://www.socialchain.com/social-minds/insights/brands-need-to-lose-their-corporate-tone-of-voice-to-succeed-on-social/)
- VILPE's draw is "office culture… personalities behind the brand". — [Sculpt](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/)
- Humour + POV as a content pillar for a Polish tools brand. — [Sempai/Wuber](https://sempai.pl/case-study/wiedza-humor-i-produkt-czyli-trzej-krolowie-tiktokowego-contentu-ktory-rozwinie-niejeden-biznes-case-study-marki-wuber/)
- Earnest B2B sales language ("feel free to inquire") becomes comedy for outsiders. — [Know Your Meme](https://trending.knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/whats-with-all-the-videos-advertising-industrial-grade-glycine-the-meme-explained)

### Inferences
- "Satisfying + punchline" and "absurd scale" (e.g. 600 kg) are plausible devices. ASMR/satisfying factory compilations do reach millions on aggregator channels (e.g. "Satisfying Videos of Workers Doing Their Job Perfectly", ~3.7M, [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WIhC6pyyf8w)). But I found **no brand-owned** example with numbers, so for manufacturers these devices remain unproven in this research.
- "Things clients say" / customer-complaint reenactment is proven in Ryanair's stitch form (a real comment as the setup). It is not documented for Polish B2B.

### Gaps
- No quantified comparison across devices beyond the single UW study.

## Q4. Which formats can run as a weekly series and are cheap for a small team, and which transfer to a rubber-flooring manufacturer?

### Takeaway
The cheapest repeatable formats in the evidence all reuse one setting and one character and take their premise from real comments. Recommended for the flooring company, given its channel rules:
- **(A) "Reply to a real comment/question" stitch with dry self-deprecation.** Ryanair model, minus insulting customers.
- **(B) Recurring CEO-as-character workplace sketch.** Raptus/HRejterzy archetypes, set in their own factory, with the CEO as the butt of the joke.
- **(C) "Day in the life of a rubber tile" personified product.** Scrub Daddy / Duo's Work Day model.
- **(D) Expectation vs reality** (home gym, installation, delivery).
- **(E) Deadpan earnest B2B pitch** played knowingly. Glycine model, "feel free to inquire" in Polish or German.

Low fit: Liquid Death-style produced ads and Dead-Duo-scale stunts (budget, risk).

### Cited Findings
- Ryanair stitches reuse other people's videos as the setup, so production cost is minimal. — [YouTube Ryanair Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/XPQdRxm5wU0)
- Raptus runs one character across hundreds of Shorts with stock situations (boss questions, expectation vs reality). Several hit 300k–1.5M. — [Raptus. TV](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC4HTatxYUa_VXPX4mi8vzzw)
- VILPE used in-house staff personalities, with no celebrity or big production. — [Sculpt](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/), [B2B Bite](https://b2bbite.substack.com/p/roofing-specialist-tiktok)
- Konesso's award-winning creatives used real team members in creator style. — [Marketing przy Kawie](https://marketingprzykawie.pl/espresso/wsrod-laureatow-tiktok-ad-awards-2025-az-4-firmy-z-polski/)
- WIŚNIOWSKI's 2.9M-view items were product-centred (MultiBox) with playful captions, so a manufacturer's product can carry reach when it solves a relatable annoyance. — [PRoto.pl](https://www.proto.pl/case-studies/czy-tiktok-to-narzedzie-dla-branzy-budowlanej-case-study-marki-wisniowski/)

### Inferences
- Transfer notes against the channel rules (no named competitors or countries as villains, no fake tests, no unverifiable numbers):
  - Ryanair-style roasting must target **the company itself or a generic situation**, not a rival or a nationality.
  - "Absurd test" gags must be clearly framed as jokes, or else use real, documentable tests. Drop tests alone get low reach (see Q2 baseline), so a test should be the punchline delivery, not the whole video.
  - The glycine deadpan works only if self-aware. Accidental earnestness is not plannable.
- Series cadence: (A), (B) and (D) can be batch-filmed in one factory session (5–8 sketches). (C) needs a fixed prop or voice-over convention. All fit a CEO-on-camera, small-team setup.
- German expansion: personified product and workplace archetypes are less language-dependent than wordplay. Stitch replies need native-language comments.

### Gaps
- No cost or production-time data from any of the cases.
- No evidence on whether a single series format sustains reach beyond ~3 months for a B2B manufacturer.

## Q5. Evidence that humorous reach led to business results, and backfires

### Takeaway
Business-result evidence is thin and mostly self-reported:
- VILPE: "more job applications than ever" (unquantified).
- Wuber: ROAS 500%+ (agency-reported, paid).
- Scrub Daddy: CEO says sales grew (unquantified).
- Duolingo: 1.7B impressions (reach, not sales).
- Glycine factory: virality brought meme traffic, not documented buyers.

No mainstream-reported brand-damage backfire was found for these cases. The documented risks are "confusing / divisive" stunts (Dead Duo) and earnest content being laughed **at** rather than **with** (glycine).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For a B2B flooring manufacturer, the most credible near-term payoff from humour reels is **recruitment and brand recall** (VILPE pattern), plus warm retargeting audiences for paid campaigns (Wuber pattern). Direct B2B inquiries from humour alone are undocumented.
- Backfire risk is highest for stunts with emotional stakes (death, outrage) and for humour aimed at customers or outsiders. The client's rule of no villains already removes the main cringe vector seen in Ryanair-style roasting.

### Gaps
- No verified case linking a humour Short to B2B inquiries or orders for a building-materials or flooring company.
- No documented Polish brand backfire from humour short-form found in the accessible sources. Polish marketing press could not be fetched.
