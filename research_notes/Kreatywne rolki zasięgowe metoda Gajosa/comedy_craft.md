## 1. Which comedic structures work in short-form (7–40 s)?

### Takeaway
The most transferable framework is the UCB "game of the scene": find one unusual pattern and repeat/heighten it. In a 7–40 s format, that usually becomes setup, pattern, then a break (rule of three) or setup, escalation, then a button. Effectiveness research adds one rule: **the brand/product must be inside the joke**, otherwise people remember the joke and forget the brand. Self-deprecation is the best-evidenced fit for a CEO-led B2B account.

### Cited Findings
**Classic comedy theory**
- "Game of the scene" is a UCB Theatre improv concept: if a scene is funny, it has a "game" the performers are playing, and the aim is to find that game and play it rather than wander. A game can be subtle (a spouse hinting they are tired of Marvel films) or broad (someone repeatedly caught in bear traps). — [The Free Association: is there a better way to say game of the scene](https://www.thefreeassociation.co.uk/blog/is-there-a-better-way-to-say-game-of-the-scene); [Will Hines: name the game seven ways](https://willhines.substack.com/p/name-the-game-seven-ways)
- One definition: the game is "a consistent pattern of behavior that breaks from the expected patterns of our everyday lives." The Free Association suggests the plainer question "What's working in the scene?" — [The Free Association](https://www.thefreeassociation.co.uk/blog/is-there-a-better-way-to-say-game-of-the-scene)
- Source text: *The Upright Citizens Brigade Comedy Improvisation Manual* (Besser, Roberts, Walsh). Its chapters cover finding the game, playing it, and support moves. — [UCB Manual TOC, Schlow Library](https://search.schlowlibrary.org/Record/424272/TOC); [notes on the UCB manual](https://publish.obsidian.md/bijou/3+Resources/comedy/Book+-+UCB+Improv+Manual)
- The game concept also applies outside improv, for example to stand-up writing. — [Funny How: using game of the scene in standup](https://funnyhow.substack.com/p/using-game-of-the-scene-in-standup)
- **Heightening** means the *consequences* of the comedic premise get worse, not that the performers get louder. One guide says ~95% of a sketch's length is heightening the central joke. — [GOLD Comedy: How to write a comedy sketch](https://goldcomedy.com/resources/how-to-write-a-comedy-sketch/)
- **Rule of three:** "two items establish a pattern, and the third breaks it in an unexpected way." An SNL-oriented guide applies it to sketches: return to the premise about three times, then wrap up. — [writingworkshops.com: how to write humor that lands](https://writingworkshops.com/en-be/blogs/news/how-to-write-humor-that-lands); [No Film School: How to write sketch comedy like SNL](https://nofilmschool.com/how-to-write-sketch-comedy)
- A generic sketch beat template runs: unusual event, a bigger repeat, a third beat that causes a real problem, a peak of absurdity, then a final "button". (Low-authority source; treat it as a template, not a Second City standard.) — [nofilmschool](https://nofilmschool.com/how-to-write-sketch-comedy)
- A Northwestern course on Second City-style sketch lists heightening, game of the scene, character and satire as core concepts, with Mick Napier's *Improvise: Scene from the Inside Out* as a required text. — [Northwestern class description](https://class-descriptions.northwestern.edu/4920/SPCH/RTVF/15608)
- **Benign violation theory** (McGraw & Warren): humour happens when something is a violation, the situation is benign, and both perceptions occur at once. Jokes fail when they are "too tame" (purely benign) or "too risqué" (malign violation). — [McGraw: Benign Violation Theory](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/PDF/Benign_Violation_Theory.html); [NPR 13.7](https://www.npr.org/sections/13.7/2016/04/25/475622815/hey-what-are-you-laughing-at)
- The BVT evidence base is lab studies (six studies, >1,200 participants, mostly undergraduates), and some scholars dispute its originality. — [Westword](https://www.westword.com/?p=40675489); [legallysociable summary](https://legallysociable.com/2011/04/18/a-grand-unifying-theory-of-humor/)

**Advertising-effectiveness evidence for humour**
- Kantar Link database: humorous ads are "more expressive (+27), more involving (+14) and more distinct (+11)". **However:** "there is no direct relationship between humour and remembering the brand, even when the humour is laugh out loud funny." Branded memorability becomes strong when "the brand is at the centre of the joke." — [Kantar: Who's laughing now?](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/advertising-media/how-to-get-humour-right-in-advertising)
- Kantar data shows humour in ads declining since about 2002, with dips around 2008 and Covid. A later update says the decline has stopped. — [The Drum, Jan 2024](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/01/08/after-decades-consistent-decline-humor-advertising-roaring-back-2024); [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/advertising-media/how-to-get-humour-right-in-advertising)
- System1/Orlando Wood: in *Lemon*, amusement and schadenfreude top the emotions linked to effectiveness. *Look Out* (IPA) includes a meta-analysis that System1 says shows humour makes major business effects more likely. **Caveat:** these are the claims of the company that sells the testing tool. I could not see the meta-analysis numbers. — [System1: A laughing matter](https://system1group.com/blog/humour-in-advertising); [System1: The serious case for humor](https://system1group.com/blog/adage-look-out)
- Wood/System1 name humour principles including **repetition, inversion, overlapping beams (double meaning) and exaggeration** (illustrated with Aardman work). — [System1 via search summary](https://system1group.com/blog/humour-in-advertising); [creativebrief: how to make advertising with a smile](https://www.creativebrief.com/bite/trend/guest-trend/how-to-make-advertising-with-a-smile)
- **B2B experimental evidence:** Swani, Gulas & Dinsmore (2025, *Journal of Business Research* vol. 200, DOI 10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115632) ran 4 experiments with 305 participants. Humour generally improved attitudes toward the ad and the brand. It was **less effective when buyers saw it as unrelated to the product** or already had an established vendor relationship, and more effective when buyers had ample decision time. Perceived humour raised intent to seek more information, via ad attitude to brand attitude. — [Wright State research record](https://research.wright.edu/en/publications/to-humor-or-not-humor-buyers-evaluating-the-effective-use-of-humo/); [RePEc/IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v200y2025ics0148296325004552.html); [Science Says summary](https://app.sciencesays.com/p/humor-works-for-b2b-too)
- LinkedIn/Magna "B2B Renaissance" (2024): 64% of business decision-makers say B2B ads lack humour, 60% say they lack emotional appeal, and 59% don't see relatable characters (67 ads tested with 1,700 BDMs). — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/creative-b2b-ads-yield-40-higher-purchase-consideration-says-new-linkedin-magna-study)
- Oracle/Gretchen Rubin "Happiness Report" (2022, 12,000+ consumers and leaders, 14 countries): 91% prefer brands to be funny, 72% would choose a humorous brand over a competitor, 90% are more likely to remember funny ads, and 95% of business leaders fear using humour. **This is vendor research. Search found no Gallup involvement** (the brief mentions "Oracle/Gallup"; only Oracle is confirmed). — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/happiness-marketing-brand-consumer-oracle/625554/); [RetailWire](https://old.retailwire.com/?p=147548); [VMSD](https://vmsd.com/72-of-people-would-choose-a-brand-that-uses-humor-over-the-competition/)
- TikTok (platform-commissioned): "Funny" is the top video category viewers want from creators. #funny/#comedy have ~6 trillion views. ~30% of users say humour in creator content is a top purchase motivation. Remixing a trend gives a 14% view-time uplift for ads. — [TikTok for Business: Why it's important to be funny on TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/being-funny-on-tiktok); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-tips-on-how-brands-can-incorporate-humor-into-their-marketing/705893/)

**Structures used by brands in short-form**
- **Self-deprecation / "in on the joke":** Ryanair's style is described as "self deprecating humor, low budget edits, chaotic personality, and being in on the joke". Its head of social said the brand chose to "lose the corporate tone of voice." — [Socialinsider: Ryanair analysis](https://socialinsider.io/blog/ryanair-social-media-strategy); [Marketing Beat, Jan 2023](https://www.marketing-beat.co.uk/2023/01/05/ryanair-social-media-tiktok/)
- A Polish University of Warsaw BA thesis coded 29 Żabka TikToks into situational, meme, absurd and self-ironic humour. **Self-ironic (autoironiczny) content had the highest engagement**, although meme/trend humour was used most often. (B2C, small sample, student thesis.) — [Repozytorium UW](https://repozytorium.uw.edu.pl/items/7e5dc5a2-bbc6-47b3-95a6-90ae3a9a78ec/full)
- **Admitting it's an ad:** a Scrub Daddy social manager (quoted via YPulse/AdAge) said Gen Z is very ad-aware, so the team leans into admitting the product is being advertised. The tone is described as "almost absurdist". — [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2022/06/23/scrub-daddys-self-aware-tiktok-videos-are-continuing-their-appeal-to-gen-z-humor/); [Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/business/scrub-daddy-tiktok-strategy-duolingo-20220420.html)
- **Workplace/industry-truth sketches (B2B):** Biteable's branded product videos "flopped", so it switched to humour about working from home and the corporate world. Reported results: 50M+ views, and careers-page visits rose from 60–80/day to 20,000+ (self-reported via Oktopost podcast). — [Oktopost: Haily Moulton](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/haily-moulton-recipe-b2b-tiktok-success/)
- **Recurring characters:** ClickUp uses office parodies and sketches, and Semrush uses a recurring character, "Chris", the overworked marketer. — [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/b2b-brands-tap-tiktok-humor-employee-content-to-capture-millennial-buyers/)
- **Characters/series beat one-offs:** "pick a person, not a topic. Audiences follow characters they recognize." Once the format is locked, "every episode is a fill-in-the-blank instead of a blank page." — [MarketScale help: employee video series with recurring characters](https://help.marketscale.com/en/articles/15265589-how-to-build-an-employee-video-series-around-recurring-field-characters)
- **Running storyline/escalation:** Duolingo's "Duo killed" campaign started with an app-icon change and escalated into a wider storyline. — [The Drum, Feb 2025](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot)
- **Industry-absurdity meta jokes (B2B):** Corning Life Sciences showed a marketing team brainstorming, where one member proposes a live falcon in every lab. Workbooks' "No Bullsh*t CRM" (a bull in a meeting room) won Marketing Week Campaign of the Year 2024, and it was not pre-tested for funniness. — [Marketing Dive: Corning](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/corning-is-latest-b2b-brand-to-try-humor/276782/); [Marketing Week: humour as B2B weapon](https://www.marketingweek.com/humour-b2b-cut-through/)
- **Callbacks/re-telling:** Giles Edwards (Marketing Week) argues for not abandoning a joke too soon, because "a good joke is always retold" (the second laugh matters more than the first). — [Marketing Week: Giles Edwards](https://marketingweek.com/author/giles-edwards/)

### Inferences
- **Brand-in-the-joke rule (Kantar + Swani et al.):** for a flooring maker, the funny "game" should come from the product's real properties (it's heavy, it's rubber, it absorbs dropped weights, it smells like rubber at first, it gets installed in odd places, the CEO is obsessive about thickness) rather than from generic office gags. This is the strongest evidence-based guardrail.
- **Mapping to a 7–40 s structure (my synthesis, not a sourced template):**
  - *7–12 s:* one-beat gag. Visual setup, then a single subversion (silent visual gag, deadpan line).
  - *15–25 s:* rule of three. Two normal beats, then a break. Or "types of customers" (each type is a beat; the third/last type is the twist, often the CEO himself).
  - *25–40 s:* game plus heightening. Base reality, the unusual thing, 2–3 escalations with worse consequences, then a button or callback.
- **Labels I added from background knowledge (not retrieved this session):** the UCB manual's "base reality" and "first unusual thing", and the "if this is true, what else is true?" heightening question. Both appear in the UCB manual as I know it, but neither was verified in a snippet here.
- Deadpan CEO plus self-deprecation fits both the Żabka finding (self-irony gets the most engagement) and Ryanair's "in on the joke" positioning. It also fits the "punch at yourself or up" rule (section 5).
- Role reversal and inversion (e.g. the CEO doing the warehouse worker's job badly, or a customer "auditing" the factory) map onto Wood's "inversion" principle. Exaggeration fits honest product demos. The demo must be real, and the exaggeration lives only in the reaction or framing.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve Orlando Wood's *Look Out* meta-analysis effect sizes, or Peter Field/IPA humour figures, from primary sources.
- No primary interview with Duolingo/Scrub Daddy social leads on *how they write* jokes; only press summaries were available.
- No quantitative evidence compares the specific micro-structures (POV vs. rule of three vs. escalation) for retention in short-form.

## 2. Ideation systems: how to generate 20–50 ideas a month

### Takeaway
Successful brand accounts generate ideas through (a) an always-on format/series slate that is "fill-in-the-blank", (b) a reactive stream from trends and comments, and (c) mining of audience pain points and objections. Volume comes from **matrices**: topics × formats, and one idea × multiple hooks. No reliable source gives an industry-standard "ideas per month" figure.

### Cited Findings
- Ryanair's social team of 8 is split into "always-on" (pre-planned content) and "react and community" (news-jacking, comment replies). The reactive side runs regular trend checks on Twitter and Reddit. "Being reactive and timing are everything." — [Marketing Beat](https://www.marketing-beat.co.uk/2023/01/05/ryanair-social-media-tiktok/); [Sprout Social podcast: Ryanair](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/podcasts/socialcreatures/ryanair-social-media-marketing/)
- Ryanair tests content live and is willing to delete posts. Staff create "in a safe space" before anything goes public. It hires people who "live and breathe social media" over people with industry credentials. — [Marketing Beat](https://www.marketing-beat.co.uk/2023/01/05/ryanair-social-media-tiktok/)
- Duolingo (Zaria Parvez): "We're very fast with reaction". Trend use is test-and-learn, "seeing if something works or [doesn't]". The team replies to comments as Duo, sometimes with a video. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/how-duolingo-is-using-its-unhinged-content-with-duo-the-owl-to-make-people-laugh-on-tiktok/); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/duolingo-unhinged-content)
- **Comment mining:** collect repeated questions, objections, confusion and emotional reactions from comments, then turn them into response videos. — [8x.social: turn comments into video ideas](https://www.8x.social/en/blog/how-creators-can-turn-comments-into-better-video-ideas)
- **Matrix generation** (sub-topics × content styles), plus mining Reddit, search autocomplete and competitor "outliers" (videos at ~3–4× a channel's baseline). Pipeline: Inbox, Triage, Scripted, Filmed, Published, with a 90-day expiry. Claims of "75–100 ideas per session" are marketing copy from a content-skill listing, **unverified**. — [viral-short-form-ideas skill listing](https://claudeskills.info/skills/vyralcontent/content-skills/viral-short-form-ideas/)
- TikTok advises brands to "know your memes" as jumping-off points and says creative risk is "respected and rewarded" (platform claim). — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/being-funny-on-tiktok)
- **Recurring-character format** turns each episode into a fill-in-the-blank. — [MarketScale](https://help.marketscale.com/en/articles/15265589-how-to-build-an-employee-video-series-around-recurring-field-characters)
- Industry-truth ideation: the strongest B2B humour comes from "a sideways look at the truth" of the category. — [Marketing Week: A funny B2B campaign starts with a sideways look at the truth](https://www.marketingweek.com/funny-b2b-campaign-truth/)
- Humour should match brand voice: "Gen Z brainrot probably wouldn't work for Salesforce, but a brand like Slack or Figma might be more forgiving." — [Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/article/why-humor-works-in-b2b-marketing)

### Inferences
- **A practical monthly system for one marketer plus an agency (my synthesis):**
  1. **Truth bank (permanent):** 50+ honest, specific category truths. Examples: customer misconceptions ("rubber is rubber"), installation fails, odd questions from sales calls, the CEO's quirks, the smell, the weight of a pallet, "it's just a floor" reactions. Sources are sales/support calls, comments, and DMs.
  2. **Format slate (4–6 locked series):** e.g. "Types of customers who buy gym flooring", "CEO tries to explain a technical spec in 10 seconds", "Things we've heard on the phone" (reenacted, real quotes only), "Drop test of the week" (real, unedited), "Office vs. factory", "Silent gag" (no dialogue).
  3. **Matrix:** truths × formats gives the candidate list. Each candidate gets 3–5 alternative hooks ("1 idea → 5 hooks"). Producing 20–50 ideas/month this way is arithmetic, not inspiration: e.g. 10 truths × 4 formats = 40.
  4. **Reactive lane:** weekly 30-minute trend and comment check. Only adapt a trend if the product can be the centre of the joke (Kantar rule) and it is still early (see section 5).
  5. **Triage scoring:** brand-in-joke? honest? punches at us or at the situation? filmable in our one location?
- Swani et al.'s "related humour" finding means the truth bank should be **product/category-specific**, not generic workplace memes.

### Gaps
- No credible, sourced benchmark for how many ideas agencies such as Jungle Creations or Fourth Floor generate per month, or their hit rates. Searches returned nothing primary.
- No primary documentation of a "customer says / we think" grid. It is a reasonable technique but unsourced here.

## 3. Hook construction for humorous reels (first 1–2 s)

### Takeaway
Platform guidance is consistent: put the proposition/hook in the first ~3 s (TikTok) or "first few seconds" (Meta), and use sound and visuals together. For comedy specifically, the evidence is practitioner advice: open mid-action or on a visually odd image, let on-screen text set up the premise, and save the punchline. Most hook statistics circulating online are unverified secondary claims.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok official creative best practices: place the proposition in the first 3 s, prioritise the hook in the first 6 s using suspense or surprise, captions at ~5–10 words per second, and 3–5 creatives per ad group. — [TikTok Ads Help: Creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Meta Reels guidance: "nail the hook in the first few seconds" (the ads guide says the first five seconds). Hook types are value promise, statement of intent, question/invitation. Reels with music or voiceover deliver "up to 13% higher incremental conversions". — [Social Media Today: Meta Reels tips](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-reels-tips-for-marketers/806808/); [Social Media Today: Meta hooks](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182)
- Secondary reporting of Meta's playbook gives 1.7× likelihood of top-20% purchase-intent ranking when brand and message appear within 5 s, and 1.5× for combining visual and sound in the hook. **Unverified (secondary).** — [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/meta-leidfaden-tipps-insights-herausragende-reels); [Blog du Modérateur](https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/bonnes-pratiques-meta-reels-efficaces/)
- The widely quoted "71% decide within 3 seconds" traces to Animoto, and a "63%" figure is attributed to TikTok but **was not found on TikTok's own page. Treat both as unverified.** — [teleprompter.com: TikTok 3-second rule](https://teleprompter.com/blog/tiktok-3-second-rule); [Stackmatix](https://stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-best-practices-2026)
- Comedy editing: open in the middle of the action, jump back, then move forward to set up an unexpected twist. Treat every clip as setup then payoff. Use text overlays to emphasise punchlines, but keep them minimal. — [Finchley: How to edit a comedy video for Reels](https://www.finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/how-to-edit-a-comedy-video-for-instagram-reels); [AIR Media-Tech: editing tricks for funny videos](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/editing-tricks-for-fast-paced-funny-videos)
- Meta also notes that branding early helps (see the 1.7× claim above, unverified). Kantar shows humour alone doesn't drive brand recall, which supports early, in-joke branding. — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/advertising-media/how-to-get-humour-right-in-advertising)

### Inferences
- **Comedy-specific hook patterns (my synthesis of the above plus craft logic):**
  1. *Visual cold open:* the strangest frame of the video comes first (the CEO lying on the floor next to a dropped 40 kg plate, deadpan to camera). The joke's "unusual thing" is the hook.
  2. *Mid-action start:* begin at the moment of conflict ("—no, it's NOT a yoga mat."), then explain.
  3. *Text overlay sets the premise, visuals deliver it:* "POV: you told the CEO the floor is 'just rubber'." The text is the setup, the face is the punchline. This works sound-off.
  4. *Sound as the setup:* a hard thud of a weight, a silence beat, then a deadpan line. Meta says sound plus visual in the hook helps (unverified magnitude).
- Do not put the punchline in the hook. Put the *premise* in the hook, and keep the payoff at 60–90% of runtime so people stay to watch.
- Brand recognition should come in the first seconds through *who and where* (the CEO, the recognisable floor/factory), not through a logo card. That fits the Kantar and Meta guidance without killing the joke.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence specific to *comedic* hooks (e.g. cold open vs. text-premise) was found.
- No 2025–2026 primary data on optimal Reel length for comedy. Platform pages give hook-timing guidance, not length guidance for humour.

## 4. Production: low-budget techniques

### Takeaway
Leading brand accounts deliberately look low budget (Ryanair: "low-cost, high-reach", "low budget edits"). Batching gives volume: set up one location once and record many videos back to back. A recurring character/series makes batching easy. For a small team, the critical inputs are a locked format slate and a shoot-day script list, not equipment.

### Cited Findings
- Ryanair: "we live and breathe low cost", and every comms move aims for "low cost, high reach". Its edits are described as low-budget and meme-style. — [Marketing Beat](https://www.marketing-beat.co.uk/2023/01/05/ryanair-social-media-tiktok/); [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/ryanair-social-media-strategy)
- Michael O'Leary (Ryanair CEO) calls social a "way to reach lots of people at no cost". — [Skift, Mar 2024](https://skift.com/2024/03/22/ryanair-ceo-shares-social-media-strategy/)
- Batching: set up lighting, angle, background and wardrobe once, then record back to back. "Most creators can film 5–10 short-form videos in 1–2 hours once everything is set up." Vary outfits so clips look like different days. Start with a 5-video batch. (Vendor blog estimate.) — [ShortSync: content batching workflow](https://shortsync.app/guides/content-batching-workflow); [Jasmine Star](https://jasminestar.com/how-to-batch-your-video-content-with-my-stress-free-planning-system/)
- Switching between filming, editing and posting is costly, and batching saves setup time. — [Planoly: batch filming](https://planoly.com/blog/batch-filming)
- Sprout Social frames batching as turning "one focused production day into an entire month of posts". — [Sprout Social: batch content for TikTok](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/batch-content-tiktok/)
- Recurring character and format: "pick a person, not a topic". — [MarketScale](https://help.marketscale.com/en/articles/15265589-how-to-build-an-employee-video-series-around-recurring-field-characters)
- Captions at ~5–10 words/second (TikTok official). — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- Comedy editing: jump cuts remove filler and can *emphasise awkward pauses or silence* (useful for deadpan), but overuse disorients. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/editing-tricks-for-fast-paced-funny-videos); [Finchley: timing in comedy video](https://www.finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/the-art-of-timing-in-comedy-video-production)
- Biteable built a B2B TikTok presence on an in-house person/face and humour about remote work, not on product videos. — [Oktopost](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/haily-moulton-recipe-b2b-tiktok-success/)
- Humour needs company-culture support; you can't hire someone and tell them to "be funny". — [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/b2b-brands-tap-tiktok-humor-employee-content-to-capture-millennial-buyers/)

### Inferences
- **Shoot-day template (synthesis; the 8–12 per day figure is not independently sourced):** one location such as the factory floor or showroom, two fixed camera setups (wide plus CEO close-up), recurring props (a dumbbell, a roll of flooring, a sample offcut, a phone for "customer calls"), 2–3 shirt changes. The agency gets a script list of ~10 short scripts in advance. Record the two-hander gags first while staff are available, then the CEO solo pieces. ShortSync's 5–10 videos in 1–2 hours suggests 8–12 polished comedic reels in a day is realistic if scripts are locked.
- **Non-actor employees:** give them one line or a silent reaction, not dialogue. Deadpan non-actors read as authentic. Get written consent for use of their image (in Poland, image rights need consent under art. 81 of the copyright act; I did not verify this in this session, so check with a lawyer).
- **Edit pace for deadpan:** cut filler, *keep* the awkward pause before the punchline, and burn in captions (sound-off viewing). A "button" (a 1–2 s reaction after the punchline) helps loops.
- **Honesty constraint in production:** any "test" shown (drop test, durability) must be real and unedited, with the comedy coming from reactions and framing. Never add sped-up or faked results, and never use invented numbers in overlays.

### Gaps
- No sourced industry benchmark for "8–12 reels per shoot day" for agency-produced comedic content.
- No 2025–2026 data on optimal edit pace (cuts per second) for comedy reels specifically.

## 5. Risk: when brand humour fails, B2B credibility, approvals

### Takeaway
The main failure modes are: humour unrelated to the product (lower B2B effect, weak brand recall), punching down, chasing trends late or awkwardly ("fellow kids"), and big-brand arrogance. For B2B, humour still lifts brand attitude, but less so for existing customers and when it is off-product. Successful brands concentrate voice ownership in a few people and move fast, but keep "safe space" testing and willingness to delete. Formal approval chains are not documented publicly.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Lightweight approval process for this company (synthesis):**
  1. *Pre-approved lanes:* the CEO signs off the format slate once, and items within a format need only the marketer's OK.
  2. *Red-flag checklist before any post:* Is any number shown real and documented? Is any "test" real? Is the target of the joke us, the situation, or the absurdity of the category (never a customer type in a demeaning way, never a named competitor or a country)? Would a long-term key customer laugh, or feel mocked? Is the product inside the joke?
  3. *Escalation lane:* anything topical or trend-based, or involving employees or customers, gets a 24 h second look by the CEO.
  4. *Delete rule:* agree in advance that a post can be deleted without drama (the Ryanair model).
- **"Types of customers" risk:** this is the format most likely to punch down at buyers. Make the last and most absurd "type" the company itself or the CEO, and make every type affectionate and recognisable rather than stupid.
- **B2B credibility:** keep a 70/30 or 80/20 ratio of product-truth comedy to pure entertainment, and keep technical claims in the video accurate. Swani et al. suggest humour works best on prospects with time to decide (awareness/consideration) and less on existing clients, so separate educational content may be needed for current customers. (Ratio is my suggestion, not sourced.)
- **Avoiding "villains":** jokes can target abstract enemies, such as "cheap flooring that cracks", "the guy who drops weights from head height", physics, gravity, or the CEO's own pedantry, rather than competitors or countries of origin.
