# Short-form video for TOFU reach in B2B, manufacturing and building materials: formats, case studies, principles and the link to the funnel

Method note: these notes rely on web search results (summaries of the indexed pages). Direct page fetches failed because of DNS errors in the research environment, so I could not open any primary PDF (Edelman, 6sense, B2B Institute) myself. Figures marked "secondary" were seen only in third-party write-ups. Anything labelled "observation" or "opinion" is not hard evidence. Search date: Oct 2026.

## Q1. Which short-form formats demonstrably work for manufacturers and industrial brands?

### Takeaway
There is no rigorous, audited dataset that ranks formats for industrial short-form video. The best evidence is a handful of brand cases and practitioner write-ups. These repeatedly point to the same things: things viewers rarely get to see (factory and process), physical demos and torture tests, a recurring "main character" employee, humour and personality, and education in an entertaining wrapper. Hard business results (sales or leads) are almost never published. The commonly reported results are reach, followers and recruiting.

### Cited Findings
**Brand cases (reach and followers; business results mostly unpublished)**
- VILPE, a Finnish maker of roof ventilation and roofing accessories (a close analogue: an "unsexy" B2B building product), built a TikTok following of about 80,000 in under 12 months. The episode headline reports "300% growth in 8 weeks." The podcast says the company "has never had more job applications." Business results are teased but not quantified in the show notes. — [B2B Better (Substack/podcast)](https://b2bbite.substack.com/p/roofing-specialist-tiktok)
- VILPE's TikTok videos reportedly passed 50 million views and attracted job applications from around the world. The company grew about 40% in two years, but the article does not attribute that growth to TikTok. — [Coastline](https://www.coastline.fi/?p=2617)
- Secondary write-ups on VILPE say it has 100k+ followers and that its organic reach is 3 to 30 times higher than on Instagram or Facebook. Its content is product education in engaging formats, but "the vast majority follow them for their office culture." These are agency blog claims, unverified. — [Sculpt B2B TikTok examples](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/); [Social Media Strategies Summit](https://blog.socialmediastrategiessummit.com/10-b2b-brands-that-are-crushing-it-on-tiktok/)
- Apex Motion Control (food-service robots) personified its products, used trending formats and pop culture, and reached 4M+ likes and about 85.5k followers by the end of 2024. The account reportedly went inactive in 2025 after its social media manager left, which shows key-person risk. — [Sculpt](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/) (secondary)
- SILVI (concrete and construction materials, a family-owned firm in Pennsylvania) is cited as succeeding through "pleasing sights and sounds," on-site and behind-the-scenes video, a "main character" creator, and big trucks. I found no numbers for Silvi. — [Sculpt / SMSS roundup](https://blog.socialmediastrategiessummit.com/10-b2b-brands-that-are-crushing-it-on-tiktok/) (secondary; Silvi follower and view counts not found)
- Siemens ran a 2023 pilot in which real employees, not professional presenters, explained complex technical topics. A blog reports a 373% ROI over 12 months, but I could not find a primary source for that figure. — [Empathy First Media / roundup](https://empathyfirstmedia.com/b2b-tiktok-pr-campaigns/) (unverified)
- Chinese factory videos: since 2020, factory-floor and assembly-line videos (wigs, dolls, gloves) have drawn millions of views. Factory owners use them, including comedic personas, to find B2B partners outside trade fairs. — [Rest of World, 2024](https://restofworld.org/2024/chinese-factory-tiktok-b2b-sales/)
- Why factory content works, per MIT Technology Review: it resonates "because manufacturing is so removed from [Western viewers'] daily experience." This is a novelty and curiosity effect. — [MIT Technology Review, Apr 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/04/28/1115876/why-chinese-manufacturers-are-going-viral-on-tiktok/)
- DACH examples (mostly recruiting and image goals, with no hard reach numbers):
  - ALPLA (plastic packaging) uses social video to counter the negative image of plastic with facts.
  - TOWA makes informative-entertaining TikToks in-house.
  - Ziehl-Abegg (fans and motors) uses employee humour sketches for recruiting.
  - A steel-hall and metal-garage manufacturer drove awareness and direct website sales via TikTok campaigns (no figures given). — [Horizont](https://www.horizont.net/schweiz/nachrichten/geht-das-b2b-kampagne-auf-tiktok-203994); [Delante case](https://delante.co/clients/how-did-we-boost-visibility-on-tiktok-for-a-company-in-the-steel-construction-sector/)
- Milwaukee Tool had about 42k TikTok followers (2024) and uses #MilwaukeeTool in every caption. A Substack opinion piece argues Milwaukee's dominance came from saturating the creator and tool-reviewer ecosystem, not from its own account. — [Stream Creative 2024](https://streamcreative.com/blog/wisconsin-manufacturing-companies-tiktok); [The Red Tool Conspiracy (opinion)](https://jacipriano.substack.com/p/the-red-tool-conspiracy)
- Hart Tools (Walmart) posted TikToks poking fun at competitors Craftsman and Ego, an example of rivalry humour. — [ToolGuyd, Mar 2024](https://toolguyd.com/walmart-hart-tools-craftsman-ego-wonka-posts-2024/)

**Torture-test classic (pre-2010, flagged as old)**
- Blendtec's "Will It Blend?" started when the marketing director saw R&D blending wooden boards to test durability. He spent under $100 on supplies. The company reported a 700% sales increase after about 186 videos. That figure comes from a Blendtec executive, not an audit, and sources disagree on the time period (2006 to 2009). — [SociaLens case PDF, 2009](https://escuela.bitacoras.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/BlendtecCase.pdf); [Wiley press release](https://gov.wiley.com/WileyCDA/PressRelease/pressReleaseId-55357.html)

**Content-type surveys (general, not industrial)**
- HubSpot's 2023 survey of 1,000+ social marketers ranked the most effective content types as funny (66%), relatable (63%), trendy (59%), authentic/behind-the-scenes (59%) and interactive (56%). This is 2023 data seen only in secondary copies. HubSpot's 2025 text repeats the humour, relatability and behind-the-scenes message without percentages. — [HubSpot 2025 trends (reproduced)](https://chartlocal.com/2025/04/10/2025-social-media-marketing-trends-insights-from-1100-global-marketers-hubspot-blog/)
- Wyzowl 2026, via secondary aggregators: 63% of consumers prefer a short video to learn about a product, and 71% of marketers say 30 seconds to 2 minutes is the most effective length. HubSpot 2026 data reportedly ranks short-form video as the highest-ROI format (49% of marketers). — [Pictory summary of Wyzowl/HubSpot 2026](https://pictory.ai/blog/video-marketing-statistics-2026) (secondary; aggregators conflict, e.g. 63% vs 78%)
- Before/after with micro-creators (consumer goods, a polish brand): one creator with 11.2k followers reached 400k+ views on a before/after video. — [TRIBE Silvo case](https://www.tribegroup.co/blog/silvo-tiktok-case-study)

**Category-specific (gym and rubber flooring, epoxy floors)**
- I found no verified viral drop-test or installation reel with view counts from a rubber-flooring or epoxy brand. The epoxy "viral" figures found came from AI-template sites and are unreliable. Greatmats (US) publishes customer-story and home-gym installation videos, but no reach data. — [Greatmats video pages](https://www.greatmats.com/neils-greatmats-story-converting-extra-garage-space-into-a-gym-video.php)
- Usable proof-test protocol: an acoustic study tested gym floors by dropping 25 kg and 50 kg dumbbells 10 times each from 620 mm. This kind of standardised drop is something a manufacturer can film credibly. — [Institute of Acoustics paper (Embelton)](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/T%20MURRAY%20L%20COSSTICK%20E%20HONG%20G%20HALL%20IMPACT%20PERFORMANCE%20OF%20LIGHTWEIGHT%20GYM%20FLOORS%20USING%2025KG%20AND%2050KG%20WEIGHTS.pdf)

### Inferences
- The patterns that recur across cases map well onto a recycled-rubber floor maker. Ranked by strength of supporting evidence:
  1. Process and "satisfying" manufacturing footage: tyre shredding, granulate, pressing, cutting. The novelty effect is documented (MIT TR, Rest of World).
  2. Torture and drop tests: Blendtec is the classic, and drop tests are native to the gym world.
  3. A recurring employee "main character" (VILPE, Silvi, Siemens).
  4. Humour and office or factory culture (VILPE, Ziehl-Abegg, Hart).
  5. Before/after installation (works for consumer-facing goods; no flooring-specific numbers).
- Myth-busting fits the "advisor, not salesman" positioning and the B2B "thought leadership" evidence (see Q2), but I found no format-level data on it.
- Reach on TikTok-style platforms in industrial categories often converts first into employer branding and recruiting. That is a real but secondary benefit, and it should be expected and counted.
- Key-person risk (Apex) means a series should not depend entirely on one employee, or that person should be the founder or a long-term staff member.

### Gaps
- I found no audited link from industrial short-form reach to sales or leads. VILPE's business results are not published in the sources found.
- I found no reliable view counts for gym or rubber-flooring, epoxy, or Polish building-materials reels. These would need manual checks on the platforms (e.g. sorting a brand's TikTok or Instagram by most viewed).
- I could not check Silvi, Hilti or DeWalt account numbers.

## Q2. Founder-led and personal content vs brand-account content for B2B reach

### Takeaway
Directionally strong but methodologically weak. Every dataset shows personal and employee profiles getting much more reach and engagement than company pages on LinkedIn. The multiples (2.75x to 14x) come from vendors selling employee-advocacy tools. One 2026 dataset suggests the gap narrows for mid-sized pages. Edelman-LinkedIn (2025) supports expert, individual "thought leadership" as a trust driver, including with hidden buyers. LinkedIn's own data shows video is its fastest-growing format.

### Cited Findings
- **Edelman-LinkedIn 2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report** (7th edition; 1,934 management-level respondents in 7 markets including the UK; self-reported):
  - 95% of "hidden buyers" are more receptive to sales outreach from brands that publish strong thought leadership.
  - 79% are more likely to advocate for such brands in the RFP process.
  - 71% of hidden buyers have little or no direct contact with sales reps.
  - 40%+ of deals stall because of misalignment in the buying group.
  - 63% of hidden buyers spend over an hour a week reading thought leadership, close to target buyers (about 64%).
  - Source: [Edelman report PDF](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2025-07/2025%20Edelman-LinkedIn%20B2B%20Thought%20Leadership%20Impact%20Report_FINAL.pdf); [LinkedIn blog](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/research-and-insights/b2b-thought-leadership-research-impact-linkedin-edelman); [mi-3 coverage](https://www.mi-3.com.au/04-09-2025/beware-hidden-buyer-flintfox-f5-marketers-size-brand-and-content-challenge-latest) (figures seen in search summaries; the PDF was not opened directly)
  - Note: the "95%" in this report is about receptiveness. It is NOT the 95:5 in-market rule. Secondary blogs mix them up.
- Demand Gen Report's write-up of the same report quotes the authors: the research "confirms the full-funnel, business-generating impact of strong thought leadership and the tangible risks of publishing low-quality content." — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/marketers-need-to-gear-content-to-hidden-b2b-buyers-edelman-linkedin/49901/)
- The 2024 edition surveyed 3,484 business leaders. — [search summary of Edelman/LinkedIn coverage](https://www.fifthquadrant.com.au/thought-leadership-research-wins-deals)
- LinkedIn video growth (LinkedIn's own statements):
  - Video viewership rose 36% year over year (Oct 2024 to Jan 2025).
  - Video creation is growing twice as fast as other formats.
  - Weekly video creation among its 500+ publisher programme members rose 67%.
  - A vertical video feed has been in test since March 2024.
  - Source: [Digiday, 2025](https://digiday.com/marketing/linkedins-video-push-appears-to-be-working-in-2025/)
- LinkedIn counts a video view as 2 or more seconds of continuous play with at least 50% of the video on screen. Creators report that average watch time is hard to compare. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/linkedin-focus-on-view-duration-signals-growing-investment-in-video-content/)
- Counterpoint: a Socialinsider study found average video views per business page fell 36% even as uploads rose. Platform-level growth does not mean more views per company page. — [Vidico (citing Socialinsider)](https://vidico.com/news/linkedin-video-statistics-australia/)
- Personal vs page (vendor data):
  - Refine Labs: employee posts got 2.75x more impressions and 5x more engagement than the company page, despite 46% fewer followers.
  - Vulse: about 14x engagement, based on 400M impressions.
  - Hootsuite 2025: average company-page engagement rate is 0.2 to 0.4%.
  - Source: [Vulse](https://vulse.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm-employee-advocacy-400m); [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)
- DSMN8 (H1 2026, 517,374 posts): original employee posts earned 9.2x more engagement than unedited shares of company content, and editing even one or two words gave 3x. Company pages made up only about 5.37% of posts users actually saw in its feed analysis. — [DSMN8](https://dsmn8.com/blog/linkedin-company-page-vs-employee-advocacy/)
- LigoSocial 2026: pages past about 1,000 followers match personal profiles on engagement rate (0.42% vs 0.43%). Comments are 4% of engagement on pages vs 20% on personal profiles. — [LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/research/linkedin-company-pages-2026)
- LinkedIn survey of 1,700+ B2B tech buyers in the US, UK and India (2024): 63% say short-form social video from industry experts helps inform buying decisions (US 59%, UK 49%, India 66%). The base was buyers who already engage with influencers, 91% of the sample. — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/social-video-influencing-b2b-buyers/)

### Inferences
- For LinkedIn reach, founder and expert profiles (owner, technologist, installer) should carry most short-form video. The company page should act as an archive and an ad source, using Thought Leader-style amplification of personal posts. This is a practitioner consensus, not a controlled finding.
- Edelman's "hidden buyers" fit gym procurement well: owners, chain ops, facility managers, trainers, and accountants who influence the decision without talking to sales. Expert, useful, non-salesy video is a way to reach them.
- The UK figure (49%) suggests European B2B buyers may be less video-influenced than US or India buyers. Poland is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- I found no Poland-specific data on B2B video influence or founder content.
- I found no controlled study isolating founder-led vs brand-account video, as opposed to text posts.
- Edelman's 2025 figures were not checked against the PDF text (fetch blocked).

## Q3. The role of TOFU reach content in B2B buying, and how to judge it when most viewers are out of market

### Takeaway
The strongest framework is Ehrenberg-Bass and the LinkedIn B2B Institute: at any moment only a small share of buyers are in market (the 95:5 heuristic). Growth comes from mental availability, meaning being thought of in buying situations (category entry points). The 6sense 2025 data shows why: the eventual winner is usually on the Day-1 shortlist before any vendor contact. So reach content should be judged on memory and attention among the right audience over long time frames, not on immediate leads.

### Cited Findings
- 95:5 rule: roughly 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time, so most people reached won't buy for months or years. The illustration: firms switch major providers about every 5 years, so about 20% per year and about 5% per quarter. The rule is attributed to John Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass) and was published by the LinkedIn B2B Institute in 2021. It is a heuristic, and the actual ratio depends on the purchase cycle. — [Marketing Week (Weinberg & Lombardo)](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule); [Revnew summary](https://revnew.com/blog/95-5-rule-b2b)
- How B2B Brands Grow (B2B Institute and Ehrenberg-Bass; Romaniuk, Sharp, Dawes et al., first report Nov 2023):
  - Growth = mental availability (easily thought of in buying situations) + physical availability (easy to buy).
  - Category entry points are the cues buyers use to retrieve brands from memory, such as motives, emotions and situations.
  - Source: [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/how-b2b-brands-grow); [CEP paper PDF (Romaniuk, 2022)](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/lem/business/en/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2bi-cepinb2b-final.pdf); [Ehrenberg-Bass B2B reports](https://marketingscience.info/b2b-reports/)
- Physical availability gap: B2B brands appear in only 3 to 4 marketing or sales channels on average, while buyers use an average of 32 touchpoints (as reported by Marketing Week; not checked against the original). — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/b2b-growth-physical-availability/)
- Peter Weinberg (via WARC): the brand that comes to mind is usually the brand that gets bought. — [WARC event report](https://cdn.warc.com/content/article/event-reports/how-b2b-brands-grow-insights-from-the-b2b-institute-and-the-ehrenberg-bass-institute-for-marketing-science/138658)
- 6sense 2025 Buyer Experience Report (about 4,000 buyers across NA, EMEA and APAC; minimum spend $25k):
  - First seller contact happens at 61% of the journey (69% in 2024). UK and Ireland: 57%.
  - The winning vendor is on the Day-1 shortlist 95% of the time, and about 4 in 5 deals go to the pre-contact favourite.
  - The average cycle is 10.1 months.
  - Source: [6sense report page](https://6sense.com/report/buyer-experience/); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en); [MarketOne UK&I](https://www.marketone.com/b2b-buyer-study-25)
- Brand vs activation budget split for B2B: about 46% brand / 54% activation is often cited (Binet & Field / B2B Institute, 2019, an older classic). Attribution and the exact ratio are disputed, and Byron Sharp criticises such rules. Brand effects take at least 6 months to show, and few B2B marketers measure beyond that window. — [WARC](https://cdn.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/b2b-marketers-need-to-enhance-brand-building/44394); [BlckAlpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-fundamentals-strategy/brand-vs-performance-marketing-95-5-rule)
- "Dark social" and influence before contact: Edelman 2025 finds 71% of hidden buyers have little or no contact with sales (see Q2). — [Edelman PDF](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2025-07/2025%20Edelman-LinkedIn%20B2B%20Thought%20Leadership%20Impact%20Report_FINAL.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gym-flooring purchases are infrequent: new club, refit, expansion, or a new home gym. The in-market share at any moment is probably well under 5% for clubs. Reels therefore need to build memory around category entry points such as:
  - "opening a club"
  - "noise complaints from neighbours"
  - "dropping deadlifts"
  - "floor worn after X years"
  - "budget for fitting out"
  - "a sustainable/recycled material for the tender"
- Suggested scorecard for TOFU reels (inference, built on the frameworks above):
  1. Reach among the right audience: geography, job titles, follower growth among club owners and trainers.
  2. Attention: 3-second hold, average watch time, completion.
  3. Distinctive brand assets present on screen, so the viewer knows who it was.
  4. Saves and shares, plus "dark social" signals such as DMs and "I saw your video" mentions in sales calls. Add a "how did you hear about us" field to forms and calls.
  5. Long-run outcomes measured over 6 to 12 months: branded search, direct traffic, inbound enquiries, share of shortlist.
- Do not judge individual reels on leads. Judge series on cumulative reach and memory.

### Gaps
- I found no B2B Institute or Ehrenberg-Bass study specific to short-form video creative.
- I found no CEP research for fitness or flooring. Their CEPs would need primary research, e.g. sales-call mining.

## Q4. Bridging reach reels to mid and bottom funnel without killing reach

### Takeaway
The evidence base is mostly practitioner guidance. The consistent pattern: keep organic reels value-first with soft CTAs, and do the "bridge" through paid retargeting of video viewers, sequenced from problem to solution to proof. Episodic series and personal profiles carry the relationship. LinkedIn supports retargeting by video completion (25/50/75/97-100%).

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn lets advertisers retarget users who watched 25, 50, 75 or 100% of video ads. This is an older feature, not new in 2025. — [WebMechanix](https://webmechanix.com/linkedin-new-engagement-capabilities); [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/linkedin-retargeting-features-audience-network)
- Sequenced video funnels: "show the problem, then the solution, then the proof," treating watch behaviour as an intent signal (agency guide). — [Single Grain](https://www.singlegrain.com/?p=74224)
- About 300 qualifying video views are reportedly needed before a LinkedIn retargeting audience starts serving. This is unverified practitioner advice; check current LinkedIn docs. — [search summary of practitioner guides](https://nav43.com/blog/linkedin-video-ads-benchmarks-specs-strategies-for-2025/)
- Rocket Agency's 2025 audit: brands using fast, simple video storytelling, especially from subject-matter experts or customer-facing staff, see stronger engagement across the funnel. It also cites a study of 13k B2B LinkedIn ads showing up to 30% higher view-through rates (agency data). — [Rocket Agency](https://rocketagency.com.au/?p=6326)
- LinkedIn is adding CTV and video ad tools (including BrandLink, which places ads alongside publisher and creator video) to win B2B budgets. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/linkedin-adds-ctv-video-tools-win-b2b-ad-dollars); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/linkedin-is-going-to-start-sharing-ad-revenue-with-creators-so-get-ready-for-more-cringeworthy-posts)
- Delante: TikTok campaigns for a steel-building manufacturer drove both awareness and direct website sales (no numbers). — [Delante](https://delante.co/clients/how-did-we-boost-visibility-on-tiktok-for-a-company-in-the-steel-construction-sector/)

### Inferences
- A practical bridge for a two-brand setup (inference):
  - Organic reels carry no hard CTA, or at most "full test on the channel / link in comments."
  - Build Meta, LinkedIn and TikTok custom audiences of 50%+ viewers.
  - Retarget B2B viewers with a mid-funnel asset: a long-form test video, a TCO calculator, a sample pack, or a "how to choose a gym floor" guide.
  - Retarget retail viewers with the e-shop offer and installation video.
  - Series formats ("Test #12", "Myth #5") create return viewing without salesy CTAs.
- Long-form YouTube (already in the client's ecosystem) is the natural mid-funnel destination. Reels can be cut from it and point back to it.
- Separate audiences: home-gym consumers will dominate reach. Use LinkedIn and job-title targeting to retarget the B2B segment.

### Gaps
- I found no independent study showing that hard CTAs in organic reels reduce reach. This is widely believed, but the support is anecdotal.
- I found no data on Meta (Instagram/Facebook) video-viewer retargeting performance specifically in B2B manufacturing.
- BrandLink and Thought Leader Ads specifics and eligibility were not verified against LinkedIn documentation.

## Q5. Common mistakes, and evidence

### Takeaway
The evidence for mistakes is indirect. The supported points:
- Low-quality thought leadership harms the brand (Edelman).
- Most feed video is watched muted, so missing captions and slow hooks lose viewers.
- Unedited, reposted corporate content underperforms original personal content (DSMN8).
- Programmes that depend on one person stall when that person leaves.
"Too salesy" and "talking heads" are practitioner opinion rather than measured findings.

### Cited Findings
- Edelman-LinkedIn 2025 warns of "the tangible risks of publishing low-quality content." Earlier editions found that poor thought leadership lowers buyers' view of the company (earlier-edition finding seen only in secondary summaries). — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/marketers-need-to-gear-content-to-hidden-b2b-buyers-edelman-linkedin/49901/)
- About 80% of LinkedIn feed video is watched without sound (attributed to LinkedIn staff). Videos autoplay muted, and captions reportedly lift view-through rate by about 12%. Auto-captions often mangle brand names and jargon, so check them by hand. — [Storykit](https://storykit.io/blog/linkedin-video-ads-best-practices); [Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/linkedin-ads/linkedin-video-ads); [ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-video-ads-best-practices)
- Hooks: median watch time is about 5.86 seconds in an agency analysis of 342 LinkedIn ads, so the first 2 to 3 seconds decide the view. Length advice conflicts across sources (under 5 seconds to 5 minutes), and the best length depends on the objective. — [ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-video-ads-best-practices); [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-video-ads-best-practices); [Goldcast](https://goldcast.io/blog-post/linkedin-video-guide-best-practices)
- Unedited reposts of corporate content get far less engagement than original employee posts (9.2x gap, DSMN8 2026). — [DSMN8](https://dsmn8.com/blog/linkedin-company-page-vs-employee-advocacy/)
- Key-person dependency: Apex Motion Control's account went dormant after its social media manager left. — [Sculpt](https://wearesculpt.com/blog/b2b-brand-on-tik-tok/)
- Rising company-page video output with falling views per page (Socialinsider): more volume is not enough. — [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/linkedin-video-statistics-australia/)
- Platform algorithms (TikTok) increasingly show content to non-followers, so each video must stand alone. Relying on followers to watch is a mistake. — [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/why-your-tiktok-views-suddenly-dropped-how-to-fix)

### Inferences
- Likely mistakes for this client:
  - Opening with the logo or a product shot instead of the hook (e.g. the dumbbell drop).
  - Spec-sheet jargon (density, Shore hardness) without a visual translation.
  - Repurposing long webinar or YouTube talking-head segments without re-editing for vertical format and muted viewing.
  - Agency-polished ads that look like ads.
  - Mixing B2B and retail messages in one reel.
  - Judging reels on leads.
- The "advisor, not salesman" positioning is an asset for reach. Honest comparisons, including where cheaper or other solutions are enough, fit the "useful thought leadership" evidence and the novelty of candour.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study of "talking heads vs B-roll" or "repurposed webinars" performance in B2B short-form. Those claims remain practitioner opinion.
- I found no evidence on humour backfiring in B2B industrial contexts.
